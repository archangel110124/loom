# ADR 0016 — Weather is one field, and the clouds are where the rain comes from

- **Date:** 2026-08-14
- **Status:** **accepted.** Steps 1-4 are built; step 5 (drops spawning from the deck) is not.
  Supersedes the *scope* of ADR 0015, keeps its technique conclusion.
- **The one thing building it changed.** The coverage curve's threshold had to be widened by the
  fade band so that cover 1.0 means *solid* exactly. A plain `1 - cover` left the sky 98.8% covered
  with a minimum of 0.00004 — invisible in a picture, and not invisible at all once rain is
  multiplied by it, because a solid overcast would then have dry pinholes and every `rate > 7.9`
  assertion written before clouds existed would depend on where its point happened to land.
  `loom_field`'s `coverage_shape` test records the distribution.
- **What it cannot do, found by building it.** The drop layer is a box about 72 m across centred on
  the camera, so no realistic cloud scale puts a rain edge inside one frame. What this delivers is
  *the rain follows the sky as you move*; a distant curtain of rain crossing a bay would have to be
  drawn in the sky pass, and is a separate feature needing its own ADR.
- **Decision touched:** reshapes Phase 4. Does not move a locked decision and adds no post-process.

## The idea

Rain stops being a global scalar and becomes **a consequence of the clouds**. A cloud coverage field
over the world decides where it is raining; drops fall from the cloud deck; wetness, puddles,
splashes and audio all read the same field. You can stand in sunshine and watch a squall crossing the
bay.

Today `Rain { intensity }` is one number for the whole world, so it either rains everywhere or
nowhere. That is the thing that makes weather read as a switch rather than as weather.

## Why this shape fits this engine unusually well

**Cloud coverage is a scalar field, and `loom_field` is a scalar-field language.**

That sentence is the whole argument. Twice in this project a system wanted CPU/GPU agreement and
could *not* use S2's `Expr` codegen: grass placement needs neighbourhood search and struct output,
and Gerstner needs a loop over a wave set with vector output. Both had to be hand-written twins —
`loom_water::slang()` emits its own text, and grass placement stayed CPU-only pending an ADR.

Cloud coverage is neither. It is `fbm(x + wind·t, z + wind·t)` through a coverage curve: a scalar
function of position and time, which is precisely what `Expr` expresses and what
`loom_field::wind` already demonstrates end to end — an expression tree, the Slang generated by
`build.rs`, and an agreement test that pins the two numerically.

So **the clouds the eye sees and the rain the simulation feels are the same function, by
construction**. That is not a discipline this project has to maintain; it is a property the codegen
enforces. Given how much of this session was spent on exactly this class of divergence — a wave set
almost drawn from wind and floated on as a plane, a depth almost sampled two ways, rain almost
occluded by the camera — that is the strongest argument available for doing it this way.

## What it changes

**`sample_rain` gains a term.** Today:

```
rate = intensity × shelter_exposure          (shelter = S3's sky_exposure, a roof)
```

Proposed:

```
rate = intensity × cloud_cover(x, z, t) × shelter_exposure
```

Two multiplicands that mean genuinely different things and have been conflated: **is there cloud
above me** and **is there a roof above me**. Keeping them separate is what lets a sheltered spot
under a clear sky differ from an exposed spot under a downpour.

**Drops fall from the deck.** The stateless grid currently spawns drops in a box around the eye.
With a cloud altitude they spawn at a ceiling and the volume gains a real top — which also gives the
stateful version, if it is built, a physically meaningful spawn plane rather than an arbitrary one.

**Every downstream consumer already takes a position.** `wetness` is evaluated per surface,
`splashes` per impact cell, and the audio crossfade in step 5 is per listener. All three currently
read a global rate and would read a local one with no structural change.

**Existing assertions get sharper.** `loom sim --assert "rain@x,y,z.rate > 7.9"` already addresses a
point; today the answer cannot vary with x and z except through shelter. Under this change a scene
can assert *it is raining here and dry there under the same sky*, which is a real test of the field
rather than of a constant.

## What it buys

- Rain shafts, storm edges, weather that arrives and passes.
- Clouds that are causally connected to the rain rather than decoration above it.
- Puddles and wetness that vary across a landscape because the rain did.
- A single authored field driving sky shading, rain density, wetness, splashes and audio.

## Costs and risks, stated

- **It is in the determinism hash the moment gameplay reads it.** `sample_rain` is currently a query
  rather than stored state, and wetness is a closed form, so neither is hashed. If a script asks "is
  it raining here", the cloud field becomes simulation input and every rule about stable evaluation
  order applies. That is fine and correct — but it must be a deliberate crossing, not a slip.
- **The field must be authored, not emergent.** A coverage field driven by an authored seed, wind and
  a coverage scalar is reproducible. Anything resembling a simulated weather front is not, and would
  be a much larger commitment.
- **One more field evaluation per rain query.** Cheap, and `loom_field` already measures its
  CPU/GPU agreement; measure the cost rather than assuming it.
- **Cloud altitude introduces a scale question.** A deck at 800 m and a drop volume 32 m tall do not
  meet. Either drops spawn at the top of their own volume and are *modulated* by the deck above, or
  the volume grows. The first is almost certainly right; say so explicitly when building.

## What it does not do

It does not make clouds volumetric — ADR 0015's technique conclusion stands, and its limits with it:
no parallax, cannot be flown through, no god rays, no ground shadows without a separate feature.

It does not simulate weather. Fronts do not form, and coverage does not evolve except by advection.

It does not make rain three-dimensional in the cloud: coverage is a 2D field over the world, so a
deck has no underside detail.

## Build order

1. **The field.** `loom_field::clouds()` as an `Expr` tree — fBm advected by the P1 wind vector,
   through a coverage curve, with `Param`s for coverage and scale. Slang generated, agreement test
   pinned, exactly as `wind` was.
2. **Clouds render**, per ADR 0015: sampled in `skyColor`, shaded with the one extra sun-direction
   tap that produces greys and whites.
3. **Rain reads it.** `sample_rain` gains the coverage term; a scene asserts rain here and dry there.
4. **Cover drives the light** — sun strength and colour follow coverage, which nothing in the sim
   reads (verified in ADR 0015), so it stays visual.
5. **Drops spawn from the deck**, modulating the existing volume rather than growing it.

Steps 1–3 are the substance. Step 1 alone is worth landing on its own, because it is the piece that
makes the rest impossible to get wrong.

## Relationship to ADR 0015

0015 decided **how clouds are drawn** — sky shader, not particles, not volumetric — and that
reasoning is unaffected. 0016 decides **what clouds are for**: the source of the weather rather than
a backdrop. 0015 should be read as a sub-decision of this one.
